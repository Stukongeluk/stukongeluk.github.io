CapyDrive: Modernizing Theory Learning

Driving theory shouldn't be expensive or boring. I built CapyDrive to solve the high costs and outdated interfaces of traditional theory platforms using Angular 21+ and serverless technology.

The Spark: Why build CapyDrive?

The idea for CapyDrive didn't come from a market analysis. It started when my girlfriend was looking for a modern way to learn for her Dutch driving theory exam online. When we searched for options together, I was shocked at what we found. Most platforms felt like relics from the early 2010s. They were sluggish, non-responsive, and surprisingly expensive for what they offered.

I realized there was a massive gap for a tech-first learning experience. I wanted to build something that would actually be enjoyable to use. Something fast, beautiful, and affordable. That search for a better way to learn became the blueprint for CapyDrive.

The AWS Engineer's Perspective: The Power of Serverless

In my day job as an experienced AWS Cloud Engineer, I live and breathe complex infrastructure. I love AWS for its granular control, from VPC peering to multi-region failover. However, for a solo startup venture, that level of control can sometimes be more of a distraction than a benefit.

"I chose Firebase because it allowed me to bypass the 'Infrastructure Tax' of my time. The setup was incredibly easy and allowed me to focus purely on the code."

One of the best decisions was leaning into Serverless Cloud Functions. They are remarkably easy to deploy and test. Since they follow a pay-as-you-go model, there are no fixed costs. The system only scales (and costs money) when users are actually learning. This makes it perfect for a product like CapyDrive, where I want to keep overhead low while ensuring high availability.

Why Angular 21? My Comfort Framework

When it came to the frontend, Angular was my natural choice. Over the years, it has become my comfort framework. When you are managing complex exam logic and state, you can't afford "undefined is not a function." Angular 21+ provides the architectural rigor and type-safety needed to build a reliable platform.

The shift to standalone components and modern signals has made Angular feel lighter than ever without losing its core strengths. It gives the platform a premium feel. The interactions are snappy, the state is predictable, and the developer experience is second to none.

The 70/30 Reality: Beyond the Code

One of the most interesting learnings from this journey was the time distribution. As engineers, we often get caught up in the perfect feature. But building a successful product is about much more than just the code.

70% Content, Marketing & SEO

Writing lessons, creating questions, optimizing for search engines, and building an audience.

30% Product Development

Building the app core, backend functions, and essential features.

I eventually spent about 70% of my time on content, marketing, and SEO, and only 30% on actual feature development. It was a fascinating pivot. It taught me that technical excellence is only the baseline. Growing the platform and serving the right content is what truly drives value.

Ecological & Digital Sovereignty: Why Eemshaven?

Where your data lives matters. Hosting in europe-west4 (Eemshaven) was a deliberate choice for several reasons.

  • Latency: Unbeatable response times for our core Dutch user base.
  • Sustainability: The Eemshaven data center is powered by 100% renewable energy.
  • Privacy: Strict adherence to local privacy standards and GDPR within the Netherlands.

It's about resilience and responsibility. It's knowing that the infrastructure behind the product is as solid and as local as the advice we give.

The New Frontier: Scaling Growth

Now that the technical foundations are rock-solid, my focus is shifting. I've realized that being a builder is only one part of the equation.

My next mission is to broaden my skills in marketing and SEO. It's a completely different challenge: studying search intent, analyzing conversion funnels, and building a brand. It's the second phase of CapyDrive, and I'm just as excited for it as I was for the first line of code.
